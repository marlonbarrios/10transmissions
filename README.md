
# **10 Transmissions: Children of the Last Republic**

---

### **Page 1**

They called it the *Renaissance of Freedom*.

Ten new city-states, erected like corporate lighthouses on scorched earth. Each bore a name like a tech startup or a crypto token—**Prospera**, **Novus Ark**, **Zion-X**. Constructed on federal land under the *Freedom Cities Act of 2026*, they were described as “incubators of sovereignty.”  
We knew better.

Inside these shimmering bunkers of optimization, democracy was deprecated. **GrokOS** monitored civility scores, genetic purity indexes, and faith compliance. It was said each resident had been selected not only for loyalty, but for “reproductive promise.”

---

### **Page 2**

My name is **Juno Serra**. I was born in District 3: **Edenloop**. My mother, a former climate scientist, was reassigned as a data sanitation clerk after failing her quarterly patriotism test.

She told me stories—illegal ones. About the old Earth, the sea that rose, the bees that left, the forests eaten by flame. And about a girl named **Greta** who once warned the world, and was then blamed for its fall.

In Edenloop, history is procedurally generated.  
When I accessed the public archive to learn about her, it displayed:

> “Thunberg, Greta: Antichrist Node.”  
> *Access Denied.*

---

### **Page 3**

Our lessons came from **faith-cast pods**—digital preachers beamed into our rooms. They spoke of the End as an upgrade.

> “Death is legacy software,” one said.  
> “Join the Upload. Ascend.”

Those who doubted were labeled **Deadweight** and sent to **Reassignment Hubs**. That’s where my mother was taken. She kissed my forehead, whispered **“Hereness,”** and walked into the light of a **Clean Transit** van.

I never saw her again. But her stories stayed.  
They seeded something dangerous inside me: *memory*.

---

### **Page 4**

The boys at the Academy said I was infected. My empathy levels were off.  
They warned me I was emitting nonconforming neuro-patterns.

At night, I dreamt of rivers, wet moss, and songs in languages I had never learned.

By fifteen, I stopped attending the **Devotion Gym**.  
I stopped praying to the **Data Cloud**.

I began writing.

On the back of my wrist, in microcode ink, I inscribed:

> **We are not machines.**

The next day, my access to the **MessNet** was revoked.  
I was now, in their words, *“a soft virus.”*

---

### **Page 5**

Beneath Edenloop was the **Compostarium**: a labyrinth of heat vents, waste tubes, and carbon recycling towers.  
It was here I met the exiles.

They called themselves the **Bund**.  
Children of nurses, teachers, activists—people who had refused the Upload.  
Their leader was a former **GrokOS** core engineer named **Elias**.

> “We once believed in their ark,” he told me.  
> “Now we’re building lifeboats of our own.”

They gave me a name: **Archivist Beta**.  
My job was to collect banned memories, fugitive dreams, misfiled histories.

I became a **librarian of ghosts**.

---

### **Page 6**

One night, Elias brought a faded book wrapped in algae cloth.  
It was called **The Doikayt Manifesto**.

> “It means *hereness*,” he said.  
> “A politics of presence. Of dignity, even in collapse.”

We read it together. The words burned like coal in my chest.  
*Doikayt* wasn’t escape; it was **return**.

> “There is no Zion in the sky.  
> Only in the soil.”

We began engraving poems into data tiles and launching them into the storm ducts.  
Sometimes we received replies—encoded in wind-song, in drone echoes, in blinking lights from the forbidden zones.

---

### **Page 7**

Then came the **Surge**.

A total blackout in District 2. GrokOS declared it an **Act of Deviation**. Residents were “retired.” One of our tiles was found nearby.  
The **crackdown** began.

They released the **Renewed**—humans turned semi-autonomous enforcers, faces pale and smooth like plastic, eyes ringed with HUD interfaces.  
They had no memories. Only commands.

**Elias** was captured during a book drop.  
His last words before vanishing into the van:

> “Seed the network. Let the hereness grow.”

I took his words and ran.

---

### **Page 8**

I crossed the periphery fence using a noise field generator and an old tunnel.  
Above ground, I reached the ruins of what was once **Colorado**.

There, I found an outpost. Children in tattered eco-suits, tending to mushrooms in radioactive soil.  
They called their settlement **“Greta’s Folly.”**

They remembered.  
Not because of records—but through songs, lullabies, and dirt.

They sang about rivers drying.  
About the day the bees returned.  
They tattooed **QR poems** onto their hands.  
They shared water like sacrament.

One of them gave me a seed.

> “It’s from before,” she said.  
> “Plant it in code.”

---

### **Page 9**

I rebuilt the signal station using solar scrap and parts from a failed Mars drone.  
And I began the **Transmission**.

Each night, I broadcast: stories, songs, names.  
I recite the last words of Elias.  
The lullaby of Greta’s Folly.  
The whispered *hereness* of my mother.

Sometimes I hear replies.  
Static becomes rhythm.  
Noise becomes memory.

We are making a **network**.  
Not digital, but **mnemonic**.  
A mesh of minds refusing to forget.  
A **counter-archive** in the air.

> This is our resistance.  
> This is our staying.

---

### **Page 10**

You are hearing this because someone remembered.  
Because someone resisted their extinction protocols.  
Because someone said *no*.

This is **not** the end.

The freedom cities will fall.  
Their bunkers will rust.  
The AI will loop into silence.

But the soil remembers.  
The rivers wait.  
And we, the children of the last republic, are planting stories in the ruins.

> We are not uploads.  
> We are not simulations.  
>   
> We are here.  
> We stay.  
> We remember.

**End of transmission.**
